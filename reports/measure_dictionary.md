## Conventions that apply to every measure

**Reopened requests.** A reopened request counts once, not twice. NYC 311 reuses the same unique_key when a request is reopened and overwrites its close date, so the published data carries no reopen event and no way to recover the earlier close. Duration is therefore measured from creation to the final close, and a request that was closed, reopened and closed again looks identical to one that took that long the first time. This inflates measured duration for reopened requests and there is no way to separate the two from this source.

**Cohort versus period.** Every rate and duration measure here is on a cohort basis: the request is attributed to the month it was created in. A period basis, attributing to the month work happened, would answer a different and equally reasonable question. Mixing the two, for example a numerator of closures in the month over a denominator of creations in the month, produces a ratio that can exceed one and means nothing. That mixed form is deliberately not offered.

**Duration units.** Durations are fractional days from second level differences, not whole day counts. A whole day count based on calendar boundary crossings scores a request opened at 23:00 and closed at 01:00 as one day and one opened at 01:00 and closed at 23:00 as zero, which is a systematic bias rather than rounding noise.

**Null handling.** An open request has a null duration rather than a zero. Zeroes would be averaged in and would drag every duration measure toward zero, and it is exactly the slowest requests that are still open.

## Measures

### Request volume

`request_volume`

**What it is.** Count of service requests created within the period. One request counts once, on the month it was created, regardless of when or whether it closed.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, created month

**Numerator.** count of rows in fct_service_request

**Denominator.** not a ratio

**Filters.** none beyond the warehouse exclusion rules

**Caveats.** Dominated by NYPD noise complaints. In 2025 NYPD filed 2,828,356 of the 3,655,039 requests in the year, so any citywide total is mostly one agency's workload and should be split or weighted before it is compared across agencies.

### Closed volume

`closed_volume`

**What it is.** Count of requests from the created month cohort that have a close timestamp as at the build instant.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, created month

**Numerator.** count of rows where is_closed

**Denominator.** not a ratio

**Filters.** none

**Caveats.** Counted on a cohort basis, meaning the request is attributed to the month it was created in, not the month it was closed in. A period basis count, closures that happened during the month, is a different number and is not what this is.

### Closure rate

`closure_rate`

**What it is.** Share of a created month cohort that has closed as at the build instant.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, created month

**Numerator.** count of requests where is_closed

**Denominator.** count of all requests created in that month, open and closed alike. Not the count of requests with a known outcome, and not the count of closures observed during the month.

**Filters.** none

**Caveats.** Biased upward for old cohorts and downward for young ones, because a cohort created last week has had a week to close while one created a year ago has had a year. Comparable only between cohorts of equal maturity. Use closure_rate_at_maturity for any comparison across months.

### Closure rate at maturity

`closure_rate_at_maturity`

**What it is.** Share of a created month cohort that closed within the maturity window of being created, counting only cohorts old enough to have had the full window available.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, created month

**Numerator.** count of requests that closed within maturity_window_days of their own created timestamp

**Denominator.** count of all requests created in that month, provided the whole month is itself older than maturity_window_days at the build instant. Cohorts younger than that are reported as null rather than as a low number.

**Filters.** cohort month must be fully mature at the build instant

**Caveats.** This is the closure measure that is safe to compare across months. It answers "what share closed within 90 days" rather than "what share has closed so far", so it does not reward a cohort for being old.

### Mean days to close, naive

`mean_days_to_close`

**What it is.** Mean elapsed days from created to closed, over requests that have closed.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, created month

**Numerator.** sum of days_to_close over closed requests

**Denominator.** count of closed requests, which excludes every still open request

**Filters.** is_closed

**Caveats.** This is the biased measure the memo is about, kept deliberately so the bias can be shown rather than described. Recent months look faster than old ones because the slow requests in a recent cohort have not closed yet and so contribute nothing to either the numerator or the denominator. Do not publish this as a trend.

### Mean days to close, maturity corrected

`mean_days_to_close_matured`

**What it is.** Mean elapsed days from created to closed, counting only closures that happened within the maturity window, and only for cohorts old enough that the full window has elapsed. Requests still open at the end of the window are excluded from the mean but are reported alongside it as the unresolved share, so the excluded mass stays visible.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, created month

**Numerator.** sum of days_to_close over requests that closed within maturity_window_days of being created

**Denominator.** count of requests that closed within maturity_window_days of being created, for cohorts fully older than the window

**Filters.** cohort fully mature, closure occurred inside the window

**Caveats.** Every cohort is measured over the same fixed observation window, so the series is comparable month to month. It is not the same quantity as the naive mean and will usually be lower, because it truncates the long tail rather than waiting for it. Read it together with unresolved_share_at_maturity, which reports the mass that was truncated.

### Median days to close

`median_days_to_close`

**What it is.** Median elapsed days from created to closed, over closed requests.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, created month

**Numerator.** not a ratio

**Denominator.** not a ratio

**Filters.** is_closed

**Caveats.** Carries the same cohort maturity bias as the mean, and is reported because 311 durations are heavily right skewed, so the median and the mean answer different questions. The median is robust to the tail, which also means it hides it.

### Unresolved share at maturity

`unresolved_share_at_maturity`

**What it is.** Share of a mature cohort that had still not closed by the end of the maturity window.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, created month

**Numerator.** count of requests not closed within maturity_window_days of being created

**Denominator.** count of all requests in the cohort, for mature cohorts only

**Filters.** cohort fully mature

**Caveats.** This is the honest companion to mean_days_to_close_matured. The corrected mean truncates the slow tail, and this number says how much tail was truncated. An agency with a fast corrected mean and a high unresolved share is not fast, it is fast on the easy half.

### Open backlog

`backlog_open_count`

**What it is.** Count of requests created on or before the build instant that had no close timestamp as at that instant.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, as at the build instant

**Numerator.** count of rows where not is_closed

**Denominator.** not a ratio

**Filters.** none

**Caveats.** A point in time stock, not a flow, so it cannot be summed across months. It is also bounded by the ingest window: a request created before the window opened and still open today is genuinely in the city's backlog but is not in this warehouse, so this understates true backlog by an unknown amount that grows the shorter the window is.

### On time rate

`on_time_rate`

**What it is.** Share of requests with a known target outcome that met the target.

**Grain.** agency, complaint type, borough, created month

**Numerator.** count of requests closed within sla_target_days of creation

**Denominator.** count of requests whose outcome against target is already known, which is closed requests plus still open requests that have already breached the target. Still open requests that are inside the target are excluded from both sides, because whether they will meet it is genuinely not yet determined.

**Filters.** is_within_target is not null

**Caveats.** The target is a single citywide number from config, currently 30 days. Real NYC 311 targets are set per agency and per complaint type and this project does not encode that table, so this measure is directionally useful and not a compliance figure. Treating an open but already breached request as a failure is a deliberate choice: excluding it would let an agency improve its rate by simply never closing anything.
