# BI handoff

This is the data half of a dashboard. No dashboard was built.

That is worth stating first and plainly, because a portfolio project that
implies a dashboard exists and then cannot show one is worse than one that never
claimed it. What is delivered here is the table a dashboard would sit on, an
export sized to open without a database connection, and the specification of
what should go on it. Someone with Power BI, Tableau or Looker Studio open could
build the thing from this document in an afternoon.

## What ships

| Artefact | Path | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wide mart, CSV | `reports/generated/bi_mart/nyc311_bi_mart.csv` | Pre-aggregated, pre-joined, opens directly in any BI tool or Excel |
| Wide mart, Parquet | `reports/generated/bi_mart/nyc311_bi_mart.parquet` | Same data, typed, for tools that read Parquet |
| Measure dictionary | `reports/measure_dictionary.md` | Every measure with its grain, denominator and caveats |

Both files are regenerated by `make reports` and are byte identical between two
runs on the same input.

## Grain

One row per **cohort month, agency, complaint type, descriptor, complaint type
version, borough, cohort maturity flag**. That is 40,751 rows, and the seven
columns together are unique across all of them.

The descriptor is part of the grain and not decoration. An earlier version of
this export left it out, on the view that complaint type was the useful level
and the descriptor was detail. That was wrong, and wrong in a way worth
recording. The upstream mart is keyed on the complaint type surrogate, which
resolves a complaint type and descriptor pair, so dropping the descriptor from
the export did not merge those rows, it only removed the column that told them
apart. A filter on one complaint type, borough and month then returned four
rows that were identical in every visible column and carried different
measures, which reads as duplicated data and invites either double counting or
an arbitrary pick between them. It also left the file with no unique sort key,
so two builds ordered the tied rows differently and the export stopped being
byte identical.

Cohort basis: a request belongs to the month it was **created**, and stays
there regardless of when it closed. This is the single most important thing to
understand before using the table, because the natural alternative, attributing
a request to the month it closed, produces different numbers for every
duration and rate measure here.

## Which joins were pre-resolved, and why

The warehouse is a star schema. This export is deliberately not.

| Join resolved | From | Why it was flattened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency name | `dim_agency` | Saves the analyst rebuilding a relationship for one label |
| Complaint type, descriptor, owning agency, version | `dim_complaint_type` | The Type 2 dimension is the easiest thing in this warehouse to join wrongly. Resolving it here means the BI layer cannot accidentally attach a request to the current version of its category rather than the version in force when it was created |
| Calendar attributes | `dim_date` | Year, quarter and month labels, so the tool does not have to derive them from a date and get the fiscal calendar wrong |
| Borough | `dim_geography` | Single slicing attribute, cheaper flattened than joined |

The general reason: a desktop BI tool handed a star schema will either make the
analyst rebuild every relationship by hand, or will infer a many to many join
and silently double count. Fixing the grain in the mart removes that whole class
of error. The cost is that the export cannot answer questions below its grain,
which is the right trade for a handoff artefact and the wrong one for the
warehouse itself, which is why the warehouse keeps the star.

## Measures, and how they aggregate

This is the part that most often goes wrong in a BI tool, so it is explicit.

**Additive.** Safe to sum across any combination of dimensions.

- `request_volume`
- `closed_volume`
- `on_time_denominator`
- `days_to_close_total`, `closed_within_window_volume`, `days_to_close_matured_total`, `on_time_numerator`

**Semi additive.** `backlog_open_count` is a stock measured at the build
instant. It sums across agencies, complaint types and boroughs. It does **not**
sum across cohort months in the sense of a running total, because it is a point
in time figure rather than a flow.

**Non additive.** Never sum these, and never average them across rows:

- `closure_rate`, `closure_rate_at_maturity`, `on_time_rate`
- `mean_days_to_close`, `median_days_to_close`, `mean_days_to_close_matured`
- `unresolved_share_at_maturity`

To aggregate a ratio, rebuild it from its components. The helper columns exist
for exactly this:

```
closure rate over any subset   = sum(closed_volume) / sum(request_volume)
mean days over any subset      = sum(days_to_close_total) / sum(closed_volume)
corrected mean over any subset = sum(days_to_close_matured_total)
                                 / sum(closed_within_window_volume)
on time rate over any subset   = sum(on_time_numerator) / sum(on_time_denominator)
```

`avg(closure_rate)` across rows is wrong and will quietly weight a borough with
40 requests the same as one with 400,000. The median cannot be reaggregated from
this table at all, by construction: medians do not compose. To get a median for
a subset, query `fct_service_request` directly.

## The dashboard this table is for

**Page 1, operational overview.**

- Filters: cohort month range, agency, borough, complaint type, mature cohorts only
- Cards: request volume, closure rate, open backlog, on time rate
- Bar chart: request volume by agency, descending. Needs to be a bar chart and
  not a pie, because NYPD alone is most of the total and a pie renders that as
  one unreadable slice
- Line chart: request volume by cohort month, split by agency, top 6 agencies
  only with the rest grouped

**Page 2, resolution speed, which is the page that matters.**

- Line chart with two series on the same axis: `mean_days_to_close` and
  `mean_days_to_close_matured`, by cohort month. The gap between the lines is
  the censoring bias, and putting them on one axis is the entire point of the
  page
- The naive series must be labelled as biased in the chart title, not in a
  footnote
- Immature cohorts should be shaded or cut off, because the corrected series is
  null there and a line that simply stops is easier to read correctly than one
  that continues on the naive measure alone
- Table: agency by corrected mean days, with `unresolved_share_at_maturity`
  beside it. These two must appear together. An agency with a fast corrected
  mean and a high unresolved share is fast on the easy half of its workload, and
  showing the speed without the unresolved share would flatter it

**Page 3, data quality.**

- The reconciliation from `reports/generated/reconciliation.md`
- Quarantine counts by reason
- Rows flagged but kept, for example coordinates outside the city

A data quality page in a dashboard is unusual and is here deliberately. The
exclusion ledger is the answer to "does this number cover everything", which is
the first question any operational stakeholder asks and the one most dashboards
cannot answer.

## What this table cannot answer

- Anything below its grain. Individual requests, addresses and response text are
  in `fct_service_request`, not here
- Anything on a period basis, for example "closures during March". Every measure
  here is cohort basis
- Medians for any subset other than the rows as given
- Anything outside the ingest window. A request created before the window opened
  and still open today is genuinely in the city's backlog and is not in this file
